    Configure your fleet¶

    The admin portal is where your security team manages the computers that run the agent. It makes the keys that join computers to your server, shows which computers are protected, sets what the agent does, and shows what the agents reported. This part of the guide explains each screen by the job you do on it.

    Open the portal at https://<your-address>/admin. Sign-in and roles are on Sign-in and roles.

    The pictures in this part come from a test workspace. Names, addresses and IDs in them are examples. Some pictures were taken on a pre-release build, so a screen on your server can differ in small details.

    Words you will meet¶

    The portal uses several words for the same thing. This guide uses the first word of each row.

    In this guide In the portal Meaning
    Workspace Tenant One organisation's set of computers, settings and records on the server. A server can hold more than one
    Enrolment key Token, enrolment token The secret that lets a computer join a workspace. The agent asks for it as the "enrollment code"
    Group Group A named set of computers that can get their own settings and policies
    Settings Config The values the server sends to the agents. They are on the Config tab
    Policy Policy Per-service rules for the whole organisation or for a group. They are on the Policies page

    How every page looks¶

    Every page has one header: the title on the left, then the Tenant list (the workspace you are looking at), a Window list on dashboards, the page's own buttons, and Sign out on the right. There are no Back buttons: use the menu or the browser's Back button.

    The line under your name at the bottom of the menu says what the server grants your account: Super admin, Tenant admin · <workspace> or Read-only.

    If you leave the Config or Policy tab of a workspace with changes that are not saved, the portal asks Save your changes? with Cancel, Discard and Save. The Policies page does not ask.

    The menu¶

    The left menu has these entries. The words are the portal's own.

    Menu entry Use it to Page
    Overview See the most useful numbers of one workspace on one screen Audit and evidence
    Policies Set per-service rules for AI tools, for the whole organisation and for groups Policies
    Settings > Tenants Create a workspace, make enrolment keys, manage groups, change settings, see who can sign in. A workspace has nine tabs Workspaces, Enrolment keys, Devices and groups, Settings
    Settings > License See the licence and how many devices it covers Workspaces
    Audit Read what the agents reported and the recorded conversations. Tabs: Events, Activity, Conversations, False positives Audit and evidence
    Dashboards > Alerts See computers and people that need attention now Audit and evidence
    Dashboards > Devices Revoke a computer, delete a revoked record, change group membership Devices and groups
    Dashboards > AI Activity, Dev Tools, Risk, Users See how AI tools are used. The screens are titled Capture, Developer Tool Inventory, Risk and Users Audit and evidence
    Dashboards > Overall analytics, Detection quality See AI usage over a date range, and how accurately data is detected Audit and evidence
    Reports Print or export a summary Audit and evidence
    Join failures See computers that tried to join and were refused Enrolment keys
    Exception requests See requests for an exception to a blocked or masked message Audit and evidence

    The list of computers of one workspace is on the Devices tab of the workspace, not on Dashboards > Devices. See Devices and groups.

    Some entries are not covered here.

    • Settings > Setup guide prints commands for another product at step 3: do not use them. Use Roll out the agent. Gateway is not covered.
    • Settings > Releases has two tabs, SS AI Setup (it opens first) and AI Firewall. The AI Firewall tab lists the installers on your server's update feed. On a server that does not host a feed, the page says so.
    • Two screens have no menu entry and open only by their address: Applications and Destinations. This guide does not cover them.

    Who can do what¶

    Every person has one base role. The server checks the role on every request.

    What you want to do admin of the workspace analyst or auditor super-admin
    Read the screens of the workspace Yes Yes Yes, in all workspaces
    Change settings, policies, groups, enrolment keys and devices Yes No Yes
    Approve or reject an exception request Yes No Yes
    Suspend, unsuspend or convert a workspace Yes No Yes
    Create a workspace, delete a revoked device record No No Yes
    Create, disable or reset the password of a workspace login No No Yes
    See Join failures, Releases and the platform-wide admin log No No Yes

    An admin, analyst or auditor sees only the workspace in their sign-in. A super-admin sees all workspaces.

    The buttons look active for everyone, but the server refuses

    The portal does not hide or grey out the buttons that change things. An analyst or an auditor who presses Publish, Mint token or Revoke gets a refusal from the server (HTTP 403) and nothing changes. The menu line under the name says Read-only for them, but the chip on some pages still reads "read-write". Give people the role that fits the job, and tell analysts and auditors that their access is read-only.

    The emergency token on the sign-in page has the highest rights on the server, but the portal shows Read-only for it and disables most change buttons. Use a personal account for changes.

    What refreshes itself¶

    The device list refreshes itself every 30 seconds and the Alerts count in the menu every minute. Other screens load once. Use their Refresh button. The workspace and the tab you are on stay in the address, so a reload keeps them. Some dashboards open on the first workspace in the list, so pick yours again.

    Where to start¶

    1. Create a workspace, or use the one that setup made for you.
    2. Make an enrolment key and roll out the agent.
    3. Check the computers and put them in groups.
    4. Set the settings and the policies for your organisation.
    5. Read What the workspace records before you tell employees what the product does.
